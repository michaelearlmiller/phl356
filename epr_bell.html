One entangled pair, three stories

A spin-singlet pair flies apart to Alice and Bob, who each measure spin with a Stern–Gerlach magnet at an angle of their choosing. All three theories predict the same correlation, E(a,b) = −cos(a−b), which violates Bell’s inequality. So all three are nonlocal. Watch what each one says reaches across the gap.

The CHSH test

Bell locality implies |S| ≤ 2, where S = E(a,b) − E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) + E(a′,b′). With a = 0°, a′ = 90°, b = 45°, b′ = 135°, quantum mechanics predicts S = 2√2 ≈ 2.83. That is the largest value any quantum theory allows (Tsirelson’s bound), and it is what experiments find.

The run fires 1,000 pairs at each of the four setting pairs, on each bench, using the current choice of who measures first.

Every bench violates the Bell bound by about the same amount (sampling noise is roughly ±0.05). The statistics cannot tell these theories apart. What they disagree about is what makes the violation happen: a global collapse, a global spontaneous hit, or a velocity field that couples the particles across the gap.

The model: spin-½ singlet, measured along axes at angles a and b in the plane perpendicular to the beam. The first measurement gives ±1 with probability ½. The second then gives +1 with probability (1 − r₁ cos Δ)/2, where r₁ is the first result and Δ the angle between the axes, so E = −cos Δ. Collapse and GRW draw both results by chance. Each run of the Bohmian bench draws one initial position per particle from |ψ|² and holds it fixed. As in the standard treatment of a Stern–Gerlach magnet with a symmetric packet, a particle in the top fraction p of its packet is deflected up, where p is the probability of “up” given the effective wavefunction at that moment. So the first particle goes up if it starts in the upper half; the dividing line for the second particle is set by the first result and both settings. “Replay same initial state” re-runs with the same ψ and, on the Bohmian bench, the same positions, using whatever settings and order are now chosen. GRW’s deviations from these statistics are far too small to show here.
