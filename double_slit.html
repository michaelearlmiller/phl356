Same fringes, three stories

The double slit run on three benches: standard quantum mechanics with the collapse postulate, GRW spontaneous collapse, and Bohmian mechanics. Fire particles one at a time and watch what each theory says happens between source and screen. Then fire a hundred and compare the screens. Switch on Monitor the slits on any bench to put which-way detectors at the slits.

The model: each slit emits a Gaussian packet (width σ₀ = 0.15), evolved by the free Schrödinger equation in the transverse direction only, with ħ = m = 1. The horizontal axis is time, which stands in for distance travelled at constant forward speed. Collapse outcomes are drawn from |ψ|² at the screen. Bohmian velocities come from the guidance equation v = (ħ/m) Im(∂ₓψ / ψ), integrated with RK4; initial positions are drawn from |ψ|² (quantum equilibrium). GRW: with the realistic λ ≈ 10⁻¹⁶ s⁻¹ no hit occurs in flight, so ψ evolves exactly as in the left panel until the detector localises it. With the slits monitored, standard QM and GRW localise ψ onto one slit (chosen with probability ½); in Bohmian mechanics ψ is not changed, but the particle is guided by the branch for the slit it actually went through, which is what an entangled detector with non-overlapping pointer states amounts to. With “Crank up hit rate”, a hit is due on average every 0.6 of the flight time. It multiplies ψ by a Gaussian of width rC = 0.35 (display units), centred at a point drawn with |ψ|² weight. The result is evolved exactly by FFT. None of the three screens is copied from another. In the Bohmian ensemble view, notice that no trajectory crosses the midline: with both slits open, which slit a particle went through is fixed by which side of the screen it lands on.
